- **`El::backdrop_lens(Lens)`: glass that bends what is behind an element.** A clear slab floats `gap` above the backdrop, flat on top with a rounded shoulder: its middle shows the backdrop as it is, its shoulder pulls the backdrop in from beyond the rim, and the index of refraction varies with wavelength, so light and dark edges fringe in colour. Light refracts twice (into the glass and out of its flat bottom) and is absorbed a little along the path, so black stays black. `Lens` sets thickness, shoulder width, height above the backdrop, index, dispersion and the light its rim reflects; its sizes clamp to 1000 points. With `El::backdrop_blur` it is frosted glass: the flat top shows the blur and the shoulder fades from the sharp backdrop to it. The lens reads its backdrop prefiltered at each ray's footprint, as rune-light's glass reads its pyramid, so text that moves under the rim stays smooth where the shoulder squeezes many backdrop pixels into one; for that, every lens runs the blur's down chain over its region (five levels), with or without a blur. It draws the same glass in a frame that blends in sRGB encoding (a browser canvas) as in a linear one, draws premultiplied inside layers and counts as paint for the motion gate. It shares the backdrop blur's copy, has its own frame limit, `MAX_BACKDROP_LENSES` (16), and falls back as a blur does: past the limit, in a target that can't be copied, or on the CPU painter, the element draws its fill (and its blur, if it has one, on the CPU). Its pipeline is not prewarmed and not in the precompiled Metal library: it compiles on the first frame that draws a lens. Apps that don't call it draw the same pixels.
