- **`Lens::prism()`: liquid glass for `El::backdrop_lens`.** Instead of the slab, the backdrop near the rim is pulled in from the edge by a different distance per colour, red farthest, so light and dark edges under the rim split into bands of yellow, red and cyan; the middle shows the backdrop dimmed and warmed a little, two lights glint off the rim and a thin ring traces it, up to 1.5 points past the element. Its numbers are fixed, fitted to one reference rendering of the look (a pill with a 41 point radius): bend and shading scale with the largest corner radius, the ring stays about a pixel wide, and a sharp rect barely bends. The slab's settings (thickness, bevel, gap, index, dispersion, light) and `El::backdrop_blur` do not apply to it. It costs about three times the slab per pixel (16 backdrop reads and a 16-term series in the rim's angle), which suits pills and bars, not window-sized panels. An element with no area draws nothing. For the ring, every lens's quad and scissor now reach 2 points past its rect: a slab draws within one level of what it drew, except the antialiased pixels just past its rect that the scissor used to cut. A plain backdrop blur draws the same pixels. The lens shader is about twice the size, so the first frame that draws any lens compiles for longer.
